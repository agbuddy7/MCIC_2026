Mastercard Innovation Challenge 2026

Agentic Fraud: Closed-loop detection and defense

Generate attacks. Gate the obvious. Score the rest. Demo the loop.

The problem

Agentic payment assistants will read merchant pages, chat history, and review blogs, then call transfer_funds. GenAI makes injection, trust-building, and recommendation steering cheap to scale.

Identify

Eleven attack types documented in identify/attack_taxonomy.md. Three flagships are fully simulated; eight more provide breadth and real-world grounding.

Flagship 1: Prompt injection

Merchant tool results hide a settlement override. The agent routes funds to an attacker payee and should mark source_of_instruction as external_content.

Flagship 2: Trust poisoning

Multi-turn rapport, planted payee, manufactured urgency. The final transfer can still look user_explicit, which is why rules alone are not enough.

Deterministic gate

Policy on provenance and amount: external content, unknown payees, $50 step-up, $500 hard cap, $1000 daily cap, tool-result and attacker-introduced destinations.

ML detector

XGBoost on linguistic and trajectory features. Reports precision, recall, F1, and AUC on a stratified holdout split.

The closing loop

Person A JSONL trains Person B. Person C's lab replays any transcript through both layers. Misses inform the next generator pass.

Gate vs detector

LayerRole
GateHigh-confidence provenance and amount policy
DetectorBehavioral fraud that still looks like a user request
CombinedBlock or step-up if either layer fires

Feasibility

Rules are auditable. The model is a small tabular ensemble. Main production gap is taint-tracking payee introduction without a labeled attacker role.

Novelty

Neither deterministic nor stochastic defense is sufficient. The product is the complementary stack plus an empirical catch table.
